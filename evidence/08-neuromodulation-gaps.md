# E-08. Neuromodulation and interventional modalities missed by E-03 and E-05

> Literature brief, 1 September 2026. Every identifier was resolved live against
> NCBI E-utilities and the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API during compilation. Nothing
> is cited from memory. All claims of non-existence carry their verbatim query,
> per EPISTEMICS.md rule 11.
>
> Complements E-03 (waveform and block science) and E-05 (CRPS). Does not repeat
> SCS, DRG, kilohertz block, focused ultrasound, temporal interference,
> optogenetics or the drug literature.
>
> **This brief was written, then materially corrected by its own author before
> filing.** The correction is section 4 and it reverses the brief's initial
> judgement on cryoneurolysis. The original error and its cause are recorded in
> §4.0 rather than silently fixed.

---

## Bottom line

The gap E-03 and E-05 left is **peripheral nerve stimulation**, and filling it
does not change the programme's conclusions. It sharpens them. PNS is the
fastest-growing modality in interventional pain, it has three genuine
double-blind sham-controlled RCTs, and its total high-quality evidence base is
**roughly 122 randomised patients**. Its largest sham-controlled replication
attempt was terminated and has never been published. Its headline meta-analysis
reports a Hedges' *g* of 3.08 from **pre-post pooling of observational
studies**. The entire clinical literature on PNS in CRPS is case reports.

Of the other modalities: **cryoneurolysis is the one to argue against**, on a
144-patient sham-controlled null in chronic neuropathic limb pain plus a
randomised human harm signal. Intrathecal ziconotide is genuinely effective in
cancer pain and near-inert in non-cancer pain at tolerable doses. TENS and
scrambler therapy fail on blinding in structurally identical ways. And the
largest thing both prior briefs missed is not on the list at all:
**motor-cortex rTMS**, which carries a Level A guideline recommendation and a
Cochrane effect of 0.77 points on a 0-10 scale.

## 1. Percutaneous and implanted peripheral nerve stimulation

### 1.1 The three sham-controlled trials

**Gilmore CA et al. *Reg Anesth Pain Med* 2019;44(6):637-45. PMID 30954936,
doi:10.1136/rapm-2018-100109. NCT01996254.** 60-day percutaneous PNS, femoral
and sciatic leads, chronic post-amputation neuropathic pain. Leads implanted in
**both** arms, 4 weeks active against placebo then crossover. Primary, at least
50% reduction in average pain, weeks 1-4: **7/12 (58%) against 2/14 (14%),
p=0.037**.

Three things the paper does not foreground. The **registry participant flow
records 33 subjects started across three groups** against 28 in the
publication. The denominators are 12 and 14 in a trial reported as n=28. And
**five of twelve authors consulted for the sponsor, six author institutions
received its funding, and two authors are its employees.**

The 12-month paper (PMID 31740443) reports 67% (6/9) at 12 months against 0%
(0/14) in group 2 **at the end of its four-week placebo period**. That is a
12-month active result compared with a 4-week placebo result. It is not a
randomised comparison at 12 months.

**Goree JH et al. *Neuromodulation* 2024;27(5):847-61. PMID 38739062,
doi:10.1016/j.neurom.2024.03.001. NCT04341948.** The methodologically best PNS
trial that exists. Persistent post-operative pain after knee replacement, leads
implanted in both arms, participant- and assessor-masked. Primary at weeks 5-8:
**12/20 (60%) against 5/21 (24%), p=0.028**.

The posted registry results are more informative than the paper. **56
randomised, full analysis set 41, per-protocol 35.** Non-completion was **14/29
(48%) in the active arm** against 7/27 in sham. On the continuous outcome, mean
pain 7.26 to **3.32 active against 5.34 sham at weeks 5-8**, a difference of
**2.02 points, exactly at a 2-point MCID, not above it** — and **3.69 against
5.37 at 3 months, a 1.68-point difference, below MCID**. Study-related adverse
device effects were **9/28 active against 14/27 sham**, more in the sham arm,
which says the harms are lead- and procedure-related rather than
stimulation-related.

**The SNAP trial, NCT03783689 — the finding that matters most, and it is not in
PubMed.** A second double-blind sham-controlled trial in the *same* indication
as Gilmore 2019. Status **TERMINATED** ("COVID-19 pandemic, Federal contract
funding ended"), n=38, results posted to ClinicalTrials.gov. Posted primary:
**6/18 (33%) active against 3/18 (17%) sham**, no p-value posted. Non-completion
9/19 active against 5/19 sham.

**The replication of the field's flagship result halved the responder rate,
halved the separation, and was never published.** PubMed queries `NCT03783689`
and `"SNAP trial"[tiab] AND ("peripheral nerve stimulation"[tiab] OR
amputation[tiab])` return **0 records**.

**None of the three registered a blinding-integrity outcome.** Every primary,
secondary and other outcome was listed for all three NCTs; no allocation-guess
or masking-success measure appears in any. Since the therapy is titrated to
produce comfortable paraesthesia, functional unblinding is the default
assumption and no trial has tested it.

### 1.2 The unblinded trials, which supply the marketing numbers

**COMFORT** (PMIDs 38821535, 39572166, NCT05287373) reports 84% responders at 3
months against **3%** in conventional medical management. Two structural facts
destroy the comparison: the registry records **`masking: NONE`**, and
active-arm subjects received the permanent implant **only after passing a trial
with at least 50% pain reduction** (PMID 39247176). A responder-enriched,
unblinded arm against an untreated arm produces an 85-point gap whatever the
device does. Treatment regions included foot and ankle, but no region-specific
result is published.

**StimRouter pivotal** (Deer TR et al. *Neuromodulation* 2016;19(1):91-100.
PMID 26799373) used a control arm with **the lead implanted and stimulation
withheld**, a device-off placebo rather than a sham. Responder rate 38% against
10%, but **mean active-arm pain reduction was 27.2%, below the conventional 30%
responder threshold**. The abstract states no serious adverse events related to
the device; the posted registry records serious adverse events in **9/45 and
11/49, about 21% overall**, unattributed.

**Ilfeld BM et al. *Anesthesiology* 2021;135(1):95-110. PMID 33856424.**
Quadruple-masked, n=66, sciatic nerve for major foot and ankle surgery. Opioid
consumption 5 mg against 48 mg, pain 1.1 against 3.1. Genuinely well designed,
but this is **acute post-operative pain**, not chronic focal neuropathic pain,
and should not be cited as if it were.

### 1.3 The syntheses disagree violently, and the disagreement is instructive

- **D'Souza RS et al. *Reg Anesth Pain Med* 2025. PMID 41193385.** 106 studies,
  9,272 patients, Hedges' *g* **3.08 (95% CI 2.68-3.48)** at 6 months. This is
  **pre-post pooling dominated by observational studies**; the authors say so
  and rate GRADE **low**. An effect size of three standard deviations is a
  signature of uncontrolled within-group pooling, not of a treatment effect. The
  conclusion nonetheless reads "support broader clinical adoption and provide
  evidence to inform payor coverage". One genuinely interesting subgroup result:
  **effect sizes were *smaller* in industry-funded studies**.
- **Ontario Health. *Ont Health Technol Assess Ser* 2024;24(10):1-131. PMID
  39886278.** The independent government assessment, and the one to anchor on.
  17 publications, **2 RCTs and 12 non-randomised studies**. Permanent PNS
  against placebo: GRADE **Moderate**. Temporary PNS in post-amputation pain:
  GRADE **Low** on every outcome. **No study compared permanent with temporary
  PNS.** ICER **CAD $87,211 per QALY**, with a **1.02% probability of being
  cost-effective at $50,000/QALY**.
- **Ilyas et al. *Neuromodulation* 2023;26(7):1276-94. PMID 37436342**, which
  restricted to high-quality RCTs with at least 1-year follow-up: **percutaneous
  PNS does not appear in its list of recommended therapies at all.**
- **There is no Cochrane review of peripheral nerve stimulation for pain.**
  Query `Cochrane Database Syst Rev[ta] AND ("peripheral nerve
  stimulation"[ti])` returns **0**.

The two society guidelines (PMIDs 41245476, 39565237) should be read as
advocacy. One states in its own abstract that its aim is "expanding access to
therapy" and serving "payors", and its disclosure block names essentially every
manufacturer across essentially every author.

### 1.4 Harms, explantation and durability

Two independent FDA MAUDE analyses converge and both contradict the "modest
complications" framing:

- **PMID 41097862**, 1,149 unique reports over ten years: procedural
  complications 24.0%, of which **infections were 26.8%**; lead migration 10.5%,
  erosion 8.09%. **Explant was the most common management, 46.3%**, against
  surgical revision 17.7%. Verbatim: "Infections made up a larger proportion of
  complications than previously reported."
- **PMID 40178466**, 594 reports from 2023: infection 22.7%, migration 14.7%,
  erosion 9.4%, **explantation 43.8% against revision 29.5%**.

On durability after lead removal — the entire commercial premise of 60-day PNS —
the only controlled data are the Goree registry postings: active-arm mean pain
**3.32 at end of treatment, 4.04 at 6 months, 4.15 at 12 months**, with no
control arm past 3 months. **Trialling before permanent implant confers no
benefit** (PMID 40659443, n=130, between-group difference 15.24%, 95% CI −0.32
to 30.80, p=0.052).

### 1.5 PNS in CRPS, and in post-surgical focal foot pain

**There is no randomised trial of PNS in CRPS anywhere.** The clinical
literature is:

- **Gutierrez GJ, Zurn CA, Crosby ND. *Cureus* 2024;16(2):e54458. PMID
  38510888.** Three patients with **CRPS type I of the foot**, tibial and common
  peroneal nerves targeted at the popliteal fossa with 60-day percutaneous
  leads, relief reported sustained 8-10 months in two and 34 months in the
  third. **Two of three authors are employees of the device manufacturer.** This
  is n=3, uncontrolled, company-authored, and it is the single most directly
  applicable publication in existence to a foot CRPS presentation.
- PMID 42456068, three further cases, no declared conflict. Assorted single-case
  reports: PMIDs 41779992, 41362538, 41031866, 41480460, 42066256.

The one registered attempt to study PNS in chronic post-surgical pain was
abandoned: **NCT04670042 is WITHDRAWN, n=0, "Difficulty enrolling in this
patient population."**

## 2. TENS

**Gibson W, Wand BM, O'Connell NE. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*
2017;9(9):CD011976. PMID 28905362.** 15 studies, 724 participants. Only TENS
against sham was poolable: 5 studies, n=207, **MD −1.58 (95% CI −2.08 to
−1.09)**, **GRADE very low**, 11/15 studies at high risk of bias. Verbatim: "we
were unable to confidently state whether TENS is effective for pain control in
people with neuropathic pain." **This review has never been updated.**

**The sham problem is not a limitation, it is the mechanism.** With **active**
TENS, **93% of investigators and 87% of subjects correctly identified the
allocation** (PMID 19945354). Of 202 placebo groups across 381 TENS RCTs, **155
used a device delivering no current at all** (PMID 35744066). Analgesia scales
with current amplitude (PMID 21481649). **The dose that works is the dose that
unblinds.**

The strongest counterweight (PMID 35144946, 381 RCTs, 24,532 participants, SMD
−0.96, moderate certainty) measures the effect **during or immediately after**
stimulation, restricted eligibility to strong non-painful TENS at the pain site,
and its author discloses payments from five manufacturers while including his
own trials.

The best-powered blinded chronic-pain RCT is FAST (PMID 31738014, n=301):
movement-evoked pain **−1.0 (95% CI −1.8 to −0.2)**, below a 2-point MCID, while
PGIC improved in **70% against 31%**. That divergence between a null-ish
continuous outcome and a large global-impression outcome is the fingerprint of
imperfect blinding.

**In CRPS:** PMID 40660498, 517 records screened to June 2025, **2 RCTs, 38
participants total**. Verbatim: "There was insufficient evidence to judge the
efficacy of TENS." **In focal post-surgical foot neuropathic pain: 0 records.**

TENS is safe, cheap and reversible. It is not established for this indication
and after thirty years it is unlikely to become so under blinding.

## 3. Scrambler therapy

**The two most rigorously blinded trials are negative**, and the pattern of who
reports what is the story.

- **PMID 30714486, NCT02111174, triple-masked, n=35.** Verbatim: "There were no
  significant differences between the sham and the 'real' ST group at day 10,
  28, 60, or 90, for average pain, the BPI, or EORTC CIPN-20."
- **PMID 32074335, NCT02701075, triple-masked, n=47, chronic neuropathic
  extremity pain.** "ST is no better than sham treatment in decreasing pain."
  Critically, **both arms achieved clinically meaningful, one-month-durable
  improvement, and 90% of the blinded sample called the treatment a partial or
  complete success.**
- **NCT01261780**, triple-masked, n=14: results posted, **no analyses posted,
  never published.**

Against these sit small positive trials (PMIDs 32269109, 25572279, 35893347,
39308135). **None of the positive sham trials reports a between-group effect
size with a confidence interval in its abstract; the negative ones do.**

The pivotal supporting trial (PMID 21763099, VAS 8.0 to 0.7) is unblinded, has
no attention control, and its first author is the **device inventor and patent
holder**, who also co-authors reviews and publishes rebuttal letters against
papers reporting weaker results, including **against the negative sham RCT**
(PMID 32441310).

Meta-analysis: PMID 36288579, 7 RCTs, **SMD −0.85 (95% CI −1.66 to −0.03),
I²=89.5%**, and no subgroup remained significant. PMID 41766048: "Scrambler
therapy does not yet meet statistical criteria for a successful treatment."

**No phase III trial exists or is registered.** The largest dataset in the field
— **NCT01347723, 194 participants, completed December 2015** — has never been
reported in any form. **In CRPS: zero randomised trials, four uncontrolled
records.**

Safety is genuinely excellent, 0.26% composite complication rate over 1,152
patients (PMID 36069623). That is the only strong claim the modality can make.

## 4. Cryoneurolysis — argue against

### 4.0 A correction, recorded rather than hidden

**This section originally said the only adequately sized sham-controlled
cryoneurolysis RCT was in knee osteoarthritis, and that the best-masked trial in
a pain indication was n=20. That was wrong**, and it understated the evidence in
the direction that matters. The cause was under-searching: the section was
drafted before a delegated search returned. The corrected section leads with the
trial that should have led it.

### 4.1 The trial that settles it

> **Ilfeld BM, Smith CR, Turan A, Mariano ER, Miller ME, Fisher RL, Trescot AM,
> Cohen SP, Eisenach JC, Sessler DI, Prologo JD, Mascha EJ, Liu L, Gabriel RA;
> PAINfRE Investigators. "Ultrasound-guided Percutaneous Cryoneurolysis to Treat
> Chronic Postamputation Phantom Limb Pain: A Multicenter Randomized Controlled
> Trial." *Anesthesiology* 2023;138(1):82-97. PMID 36512721,
> doi:10.1097/ALN.0000000000004429, PMC10374196. NCT03449667.**

Multicentre, randomised, sham-controlled, publicly funded. Every participant
first received a femoral and sciatic block, then was randomised to
cryoneurolysis or sham **at those same locations** — a genuinely good sham
architecture. **n=144.**

**Primary outcome, change in average phantom pain at 4 months: −0.5 [−0.5, 3.0]
active against 0 [0, 3] sham; estimated difference −0.1 (95% CI −1.0 to +0.7),
p=0.759.**

The single serious adverse event was a protocol deviation in which **a femoral
nerve cryolesion below the inguinal ligament, instead of the sensory-only
saphenous nerve, produced quadriceps weakness, a possible fall and a clavicle
fracture.**

This is the largest sham-controlled cryoneurolysis trial in any pain indication,
it is in **chronic neuropathic pain of a lower limb**, and it is flatly negative.

### 4.2 The harm signal is human and randomised, not merely rodent

- **Ju H, Feng Y, Yang BX, Wang J. *Eur J Pain* 2008;12(3):378-84. PMID
  17870625.** RCT, n=107, epidural against intercostal cryoanalgesia, followed
  12 months. Verbatim: **"The incidence of allodynia-like pain reported in cryo
  group was higher than that in Epidural group on any postoperative month, with
  significance on the sixth and the twelfth months postoperatively (P<0.05)."**
  Authors' conclusion: **"Cryoanalgesia may be a factor that increases the
  incidence of neuropathic pain."**
- **Humble SR, Dalton AJ, Li L. *Eur J Pain* 2015;19(4):451-65. PMID 25088289.**
  32 RCTs: intercostal cryoanalgesia "was not effective and has the potential to
  increase the risk of chronic pain."
- **DeLeo JA et al. *Pain* 1994;56(1):9-16. PMID 8159445.** Cryoneurolysis is a
  **named, validated rodent model of neuropathic pain**, and **autotomy peaks
  when sensation is returning** — the pain phenomenon is tied to the very
  regeneration phase clinicians rely on for reversibility. Corroborated by PMID
  10364441, in which cryoneurolysis produced allodynia and glial activation
  indistinguishable from tight ligation. Also PMID 11113289.

### 4.3 The mechanism claim has no primary human source

The claim that cryoneurolysis spares the endoneurium and therefore does not form
neuroma **has no primary human histological source.** It traces through
narrative reviews (PMIDs 16880882, 29489178) to an uncontrolled 1976 case series
with a **median duration of relief of 11 days** (PMID 62163). **No study has
used neuroma as an endpoint.** The claim is untested, not established, and
absence of case reports is weak evidence of absence when nobody is looking.

### 4.4 The positive trial, in context

Radnovich R et al. *Osteoarthritis Cartilage* 2017;25(8):1247-56. PMID 28336454,
NCT02260921, quadruple-masked, n=180, **knee osteoarthritis**: WOMAC pain change
at day 30 **−16.33 (SE 1.15) against −8.79 (SE 1.63); LS-mean difference −7.53,
95% CI −11.48 to −3.58, p=0.0002** on a 0-50 scale — about 15% of scale range,
at rather than above the boundary of clinical importance. A randomised
head-to-head now shows **cooled radiofrequency ablation superior to
cryoneurolysis at 6 months** (PMID 41487835), with the cryo arm back near
baseline. Knee osteoarthritis is a nociceptive indication and does not transfer.

**In CRPS: two case reports** (PMIDs 42484550, 34528377). Nothing else.

**Publication and attrition bias in the commercial programme is a live,
unresolved risk:** NCT04191031 is **TERMINATED at n=79** because an interim
stopping criterion was met, **with no results posted** and no disclosure of
whether the criterion was efficacy or futility; two further trials were
**withdrawn at zero enrolment**.

## 5. Intrathecal drug delivery

**Ziconotide works in cancer pain and barely works in non-cancer pain.**

| Trial | Population | n | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| PMID 14709577 | Cancer or AIDS, refractory | 111 | **53.1% (95% CI 44.0-62.2) against 18.1% (4.8-31.4), p<0.001** |
| PMID 22151630 | Chronic **non-malignant** | 255 | **31.2% against 6.0%, p≤0.001** — but starting and maximum doses were **cut mid-trial** for adverse events |
| PMID 16716870 | Severe chronic pain, **slow titration** | 220 | **14.7% against 7.2%, p=0.036** — 7.5 percentage points on a 0-100 VAS, about **0.75 points on 0-10** |

At tolerable doses the effect is a fraction of any MCID. At effective doses the
neuropsychiatric burden forced a protocol amendment. **And the slow-titration
trial was almost certainly functionally unblinded**: dizziness **46% against
13%**, ataxia **14% against 1%**.

**The label carries a suicide signal**, verbatim: "there was a higher incidence
of suicide, suicide attempts, and suicide ideations in PRIALT-treated patients
than in the placebo group (**0.27/patient year against 0.10/patient year**)",
plus hallucinations 12%, confusion 33%, memory impairment 22%.

**Real-world persistence is poor** (PMID 18227325, n=644): **median duration of
therapy 67.5 days**, only 18.5% reaching 360 days, median VASPI 76 mm at
baseline, 68 mm at month 1, **73 mm at last observation**.

**Intrathecal opioid for non-cancer pain carries a mortality signal**, from the
manufacturer's own analysis (PMID 20029253): mortality **0.088% at 3 days, 0.39%
at 1 month, 3.89% at 1 year**. Standardised (PMID 20642729): **SMR 7.5× at 3
days, 3.4× at 1 month, 2.7× at 1 year**, with the verbatim conclusion that a
substantial portion "is probably therapy related."

**Catheter-tip granuloma** (PMID 11844237): 41 cases, morphine or hydromorphone
in 39/41, **none in baclofen-only patients**; **30/41 required surgery for cord
or cauda equina compression and 11/41 were non-ambulatory at last follow-up.**
Incidence 3% on short surveillance (PMID 16868596) but **8.7% over a mean 33.5
months**, in which **4 of 6 occurred at low dose and low concentration**,
contradicting the keep-the-concentration-low doctrine (PMID 27505059).

The authoritative synthesis (PMID 17237668) is verbatim: "Six articles met the
inclusion criteria... **none were randomized trials or of ziconotide.**"
**"Intrathecal morphine-equivalent doses increased over time."**

**In CRPS there is no RCT of intrathecal ziconotide or morphine.** The relevant
datum is a cohort and it is negative: of 1,653 implanted systems, 62 were for
CRPS, 26 had four or more years of follow-up, and verbatim **"Pain scores did
not significantly decrease over time, and we observed no correlation between
pain intensity and use of any intrathecal medication"** (PMID 29889241).

## 6. The largest omission from both prior briefs: motor-cortex rTMS

- **Lefaucheur JP et al. *Clin Neurophysiol* 2020;131(2):474-528. PMID
  31901449.** **Level A evidence, definite efficacy, for high-frequency rTMS of
  M1 contralateral to the painful side in neuropathic pain** — one of only three
  Level A indications in the entire guideline.
- **O'Connell NE et al. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2018;4(4):CD008208. PMID
  29652088.** High-frequency M1 rTMS, single-dose studies, pooled n=249: **SMD
  −0.38 (95% CI −0.49 to −0.27)**, equivalent to **0.77 points on 0-10**, a 12%
  reduction — **below the review's own 15% MCID threshold**, low quality.
  Multiple-dose studies were heterogeneous with **no evidence of effect**. No
  evidence rTMS improves disability.
- **This Cochrane review has not been updated since 2018**, so both the guideline
  and the sceptical synthesis are eight years stale.

**Targeted muscle reinnervation and regenerative peripheral nerve interfaces**
are surgical rather than neuromodulatory, but are the closest thing to a
disease-modifying intervention for a focal nerve generator. The one RCT (PMID
30371518, n=28) **missed its intention-to-treat primary**: phantom limb pain
difference 3.4 (adjusted CI −0.1 to 6.9, p=0.06); residual limb pain 1.9 (−0.5
to 4.4, p=0.15). Only a secondary longitudinal model reached significance. A
sham-controlled trial is under way (PMID 41248411).

**The honest cross-modality synthesis** is PMID 42677241, a network
meta-analysis of 68 RCTs across rTMS, tDCS, TENS, SCS and PNS. Its own verdict:
"most comparisons between active interventions were not statistically
significant, the evidence network was uneven, and confidence in the evidence was
**very low**."

## 7. One correction to E-05

The stimulation-holiday salvage evidence (D'Souza & Her, *Reg Anesth Pain Med*
2022;47(12):722-7. PMID 35985769) is titled "Stimulation holiday rescues
analgesia after habituation and loss of efficacy from **10-kilohertz dorsal
column spinal cord stimulation**." **Its transfer to DRG stimulation is an
extrapolation, not a citation**, and should be stated as such wherever the
figure is used.

## 8. Established / contested / speculative

**ESTABLISHED.** Percutaneous 60-day PNS beats sham on a responder endpoint in
two small double-blind trials; total randomised across all three sham-controlled
PNS trials is about 122. PNS complication reporting is dominated by infection,
migration and erosion, and **explantation is the most common management of a
complication** in two independent MAUDE analyses. Cochrane cannot state whether
TENS is effective for neuropathic pain, and TENS blinding fails at effective
doses. The two best-blinded scrambler trials are negative. **Cryoneurolysis is
null against sham in chronic neuropathic limb pain at n=144.** Ziconotide beats
placebo by 35 percentage points in cancer pain and 7.5 in non-cancer pain.
Intrathecal opioid for non-cancer pain carries 3.89% one-year mortality.
High-frequency M1 rTMS has Level A guideline status and a sub-MCID Cochrane
effect.

**CONTESTED.** Whether 60-day PNS produces durable relief after lead removal,
given the only controlled continuous data show decay from 3.32 to 4.15 with no
comparator past 3 months. Whether PNS's pooled *g* of 3.08 means anything, given
it is pre-post observational pooling. Whether the negative scrambler trials
reflect a null treatment or a therapeutic sham.

**SPECULATIVE.** PNS in CRPS (case reports only, best of them n=3 and
company-authored). Scrambler in CRPS (4 uncontrolled records). Cryoneurolysis in
CRPS (2 case reports, against a null RCT and a harm signal). Intrathecal
ziconotide in CRPS (case reports plus one cohort in which pain did not fall over
four years). **PNS as salvage for a failing DRG system: zero publications.**

## 9. A structural observation

The same two dozen investigators author the trials, the meta-analyses and the
guidelines across every modality in this brief. One senior author co-authors the
pivotal PNS trials, consults for their sponsor, **and** co-authors the null
cryoneurolysis trial. Another chairs a data safety monitoring board for a device
trial cited in E-03 while consulting for a cryoneurolysis manufacturer. This is
not an accusation of bad faith — the null trials are the same people's work, and
they published them. It is a statement about **effective sample size in the
expert population**: the field's evidence, its syntheses and its
recommendations are not independent draws.

## 10. Residual gaps, carried forward

- **PACC 2024 (PMID 38752946) drug-tier recommendations and any CRPS-specific
  text could not be retrieved** (no PMCID, publisher full text unreachable). Do
  not attribute any specific recommendation without pulling the PDF. What is
  verified is the framework: USPSTF grading, with recommendations permitted
  "based on the strength of evidence **or consensus when evidence is scant**."
- **Pooled explantation, infection and hardware-failure rates specific to
  chronic-pain intrathecal systems are not retrievable by PubMed keyword
  search.** Four verbatim queries returned 0 or only spasticity and cancer
  cohorts. §5 should not be read as having covered this.
- Full texts of PMIDs 33856424, 38739062 and 28336454 are paywalled, so blinding
  discussion not present in abstracts remains unchecked.
- Belgian reimbursement status of percutaneous PNS is `[UNVERIFIED]`, based on
  public payer pages rather than the nomenclature text.
