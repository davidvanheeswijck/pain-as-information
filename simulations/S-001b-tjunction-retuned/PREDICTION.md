# Pre-registered prediction for S-1b: re-tuned T-junction model, rate versus pattern

**Written 2026-11-17, before any simulation run of any kind, including pilots.**
This file is commit #1 of its branch and alone in that commit; no code and no
results file exist in the history before it. Intent:
`intent/simulation/2026-11-17-s1b-retuned-tjunction.md`. If the simulation
disagrees with this file, the simulation is probably right and this file is the
record of my being wrong.

What was done before this commit, and nothing else: the S-1 code, its
`PREDICTION.md` (amendments A to H and corrections), its `results.md`, the
ledger's "Simulations" entry and the three abstracts below were read; the
abstracts were fetched through NCBI E-utilities efetch; a Python 3.13 virtual
environment was created and S-1's four-file `mod/` set was compiled by
`nrnivmodl` (it compiles; nothing was run). **No NEURON simulation has been
executed for S-1b.** What is known from S-1 is its outcome (the model it built
blocked C-type traffic within about 0.56 s at every rate of 4 Hz and above).
That outcome is the reason this study exists and it is why the progressive-block
target below is stated before the search rather than discovered by it.

## The questions

Unchanged from S-1 (`../S-001-tjunction-rate-vs-pattern/PREDICTION.md`):

- **Q1.** Does stimulation *pattern* change T-junction filtering beyond mean
  rate? (Specificity: pattern versus dose.)
- **Q2.** Can a T-junction low-pass account retrodict the blinded human
  frequency ordering of Piedade et al. 2022 (PMID 35217898; n=19; 20 Hz best,
  VAS 3.7 at 20 Hz rising to 5.8 at 80 Hz) and 2023 (PMID 36705762; n=17; 4 Hz,
  20 Hz and 60 Hz all better than sham, not separable from each other)?

S-1b asks them of a model that can pass its controls. S-1 could not (NO
VERDICT). Its own diagnosis named its tuning amendment (Ca decay and SK set
against Sundt's 6 Hz) as the untested likely cause. S-1b tests that by
re-tuning, under a pre-registered search, with the circularity problem solved
in advance (next section).

## The circularity problem, and how it is split

If parameters are tuned until Chao 2020's progressive block appears, then Chao's
block can no longer serve as an independent positive control. So the facts are
split, and the split is fixed here before any run.

**Calibration targets (used to choose parameters; the only facts the search may
see):**

- **T1-T3, Sundt 2015 following frequencies** (PMID 26334005; abstract read via
  efetch): "A model containing only fast voltage-gated Na+ and
  delayed-rectifier K+ channels conducted trains of spikes up to frequencies of
  110 Hz. The addition of slowly activating KCNQ channels (i.e., KV7 or
  M-channels) to the model reduced the following frequency to 30 Hz.
  Hyperpolarization produced by addition of a much slower conductance, such as a
  Ca(2+)-dependent K(+) current, was needed to reduce the following frequency to
  6 Hz." Bands, as in S-1: 82.5 to 137.5 Hz (Na+Kdr), 22.5 to 37.5 Hz (+KCNQ/M),
  4.5 to 7.5 Hz (+Ca-dependent K), all within +/-25%.
- **T4, progressive block at regular 20 Hz, 1.5x**, from Chao et al. 2020, Pain
  (PMID 32658148; abstract read via efetch), verbatim: "Transmission of
  APs directly generated by GFS (20 Hz) in C-type units progressively abated over
  20 seconds, whereas GFS-induced Aβ activity persisted unabated, while Aδ
  showed an intermediate pattern." and "Activity generated peripherally by
  electrical stimulation of the sciatic nerve and punctate mechanical
  stimulation of the receptive field (glabrous skin) was likewise fully blocked
  by GFS within 20 seconds in C-type units, whereas Aβ units were minimally
  affected". The abstract gives a time scale (about 20 s, complete within 20 s)
  and no lower bound and no rate of onset. T4 is made operational, in model
  terms, by three conditions on one 30 s regular 20 Hz run at 1.5x with the 2 Hz
  probe (definitions of F, "early", "late" and onset below):
  **B1** F over the first 5 s window <= 0.50 (needs >= 3 scored probes in the
  window); **B2** F over the last 10 s window >= 0.80 (needs >= 3 scored
  probes); **B3** block onset (the first run of 5 consecutive failed scored
  probes) is defined and falls between 2 s and 20 s after stimulation starts.
  The 2 s lower bound and the 0.50 and 0.80 levels are my choices, made before
  any run; S-1's model had an onset of about 0.56 s and F about 1 in the first
  5 s, and the lower bound is set above that. They are a reading of "progressively
  abated over 20 seconds", not numbers from the paper.

**Held-out validation (never used in tuning, named here in advance):** two
features from conditions the calibration never touched. Both are quoted from
abstracts read via efetch.

- **V1, stimulation-frequency dependence, from Kent et al. 2018, Neuromodulation
  (PMID 29377442).** Verbatim: "the model output demonstrated that T-junction
  filtering was amplified with DRG stimulation, thereby blocking afferent
  signaling, with cathodic stimulation at amplitudes of 2.8-5.5 × stimulation
  threshold and frequencies above 2 Hz. This amplified filtering was dependent on
  the presence of calcium and calcium-dependent small-conductance potassium
  channels". The calibration used stimulation at 20 Hz only (and peripheral trains
  of 21 spikes for Sundt's frequencies); it never saw stimulation at 2 Hz or
  4 Hz. **Amplitude is not used:** Kent's 2.8-5.5x is an extracellular cathodic
  amplitude relative to extracellular threshold, and this model's 1.0x and 1.5x
  are intracellular multiples of a steady-state drive threshold; they are not
  commensurable, and I will not pretend they are. **SK dependence is not used:**
  the calibrated block is built from SK, so removing SK removing the block would
  be a restatement of the tuning. V1 is the claim that stimulation at 2 Hz does
  not amplify filtering and stimulation above 2 Hz (tested at 4 Hz) does. Kent's
  model is itself a Sundt-type model with SK, so what is held out is the data,
  not the mechanism class; that is declared.
- **V2, cumulative failure, from Gemes et al. 2013, J Physiol (PMID 23148321).**
  Verbatim: "Following frequency was slower for a train of 20 APs than for two,
  indicating that a cumulative process leads to propagation failure." The
  calibration used 21-pulse trains only (Sundt); two-pulse trains were never
  measured. It is a weak, qualitative feature and I expect most SK-bearing models
  to pass it; it is included because it is independent and cheap, and it is not
  the load-bearing test. V1 is.

**Not used, and why:** Chao 2020's abstract does not state recovery after
stimulation stops (the sentence "reversed to normal during GFS" is about von Frey
threshold, not conduction), so no recovery feature is available as a held-out
test. Gemes 2013's abstract gives no numeric following-frequency range (see
S-1); its range is not used for tuning.

**Held-out rule.** If V1 or V2 fails or is inconclusive, P1 and P2 are
**NO VERDICT**, exactly as in S-1 when a control fails. If the search finds no set
meeting the calibration targets, nothing further is run and the result is the
sentence fixed below.

## Operational definitions (S-1's final definitions, fixed here, not re-opened)

These are S-1's definitions as they stood after its amendments C to E and H, so
that this file is self-contained. None may change after any run.

- **Model.** Pseudounipolar C-neuron after Sundt 2015, as `tjmodel.CNeuron` in S-1
  (imported unchanged): soma, stem, T-junction, peripheral and central axons; Na
  (nahh, -6/+6 mV), Kdr, KCNQ/M (iM, vshift -5 mV), and, for the SK
  configuration, L-type Ca (cal) plus SK in the soma (and in the stem if
  `sk_stem`). `stem(0)` is attached to the T-junction; the junction-side stem
  recording site comes from topology (`junction_side_stem_x`). Aβ comparator:
  `tjmodel.ANeuron` from S-1, unchanged (generic node-and-internode construction,
  rest -65 mV, CV about 3.7 m/s; not a calibrated Aβ fibre).
- **Stimulus.** 1 ms rectangular current pulses at the mid-stem for 30 s. The
  *from-rest threshold* is the smallest single pulse producing a spike crossing
  the junction-side stem site (bisection to 1%). The *steady-state drive
  threshold* is the smallest multiple (grid 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8,
  10, 12) of the from-rest threshold at which at least 90% of the pulses in the
  second half of a 4 s regular 20 Hz train evoke a junction-side stem spike.
  Runs are at 1.0x and 1.5x the drive threshold. Variants that cannot be driven
  are dropped and counted.
- **Probe (test) spikes.** The primary probe is a 2 Hz train of single
  suprathreshold peripheral pulses at the distal peripheral end, each jittered
  +/-30% of its slot, identical in every condition of a model. The 10 Hz and 5 Hz
  trains are run on the 30-model subsample (1.5x; regular 20 Hz, regular 80 Hz,
  burst, five seeds each of Poisson and jitter, plus probe-only baselines).
- **F (filtering index).** Over scored probes: a probe is *scored* if it reaches
  the pre-junction peripheral site (0.95 of the peripheral section) and is not
  *ambiguous*. It is ambiguous if a stimulus-evoked spike crosses the
  junction-side stem site in a window that could produce a central crossing
  inside the success window (so that a stimulus spike would be mistaken for the
  probe at the central site); the probe's own backfire within about one
  conduction step is not counted as ambiguity. A scored probe succeeds if a
  spike crosses the central site (60 um into the central axon) in
  [te + 0.4 d0, te + 2.5 d0 + 2] ms. F = 1 - success fraction among scored probes.
  `F_all` keeps every probe that arrived (no ambiguity exclusion). **`F_all` is
  not a lower bound on F at high rates**: at 80 Hz the excluded probes fail much
  more often than the scored ones (S-1: about 0.6 against about 0.05).
  Therefore F, F_all, scored / tested probes and the excluded-probe failure rate
  are all reported next to every F.
- **Early, late, onset.** F_early is over test times in [0, 5.3 s) of the run
  clock (stimulation starts at 0.2 s) and F_late over the last 10 s; each needs
  >= 3 scored probes or is undefined. Block onset is the test time of the first
  of 5 consecutive failed scored probes (run clock), reported relative to
  stimulation start (0.2 s); "none" if there is no such run.
- **Following frequency.** The first failing grid frequency of a 12% geometric
  grid (2 Hz upward, 40 points), refined once to about 6%, by a 21-pulse
  peripheral train that must reach the central axon in full; no DRG stimulation.
  (It is scanned, not bisected, because following is not monotone in these
  models.) For V2 the same procedure is run with a 2-pulse train.
- **Patterns, all 600 pulses over 30 s (20 Hz mean).** Regular 4, 20, 40, 60,
  80 Hz (and, for V1 only, 2 Hz; 60 pulses); Poisson (2 ms dead time,
  exponential intervals rescaled to span 30 s); burst (5 pulses at 100 Hz every
  250 ms); jitter (instantaneous rates uniform 2 to 50 Hz, intervals rescaled so
  600 pulses span 30 s).
- **Dropped candidates are a selection effect** and their number is reported.

## Pre-registered thresholds and verdict rules (verbatim from S-1)

- **P1 (pattern ≈ rate):** every matched-rate pattern (Poisson, burst = 5
  pulses at 100 Hz every 250 ms, uniform jitter over 2-50 Hz; all at 20 Hz
  mean and matched pulse count) gives filtering index F within 10 percentage
  points of regular 20 Hz in ≥80% of models. **Refuted** if any matched-rate
  pattern raises population-mean F by ≥20 points with the 95% CI excluding 10.
- **P2 (no human ordering):** F(80 Hz) ≥ F(20 Hz) in ≥90% of models.
  **Refuted** if F falls by ≥20% relative from 20 to 80 Hz in most models
  (then the T-junction account is consistent with the blinded human ordering).
- **Ensemble rule (S-1):** population of ≥200 C-neuron variants, accepted only if
  their unstimulated following frequency lies in the experimental C-type range.
  Stochastic patterns: ≥20 seeds on a subsample of ≥30 models. dt-halving
  convergence check. Report INCONCLUSIVE whenever a CI spans its threshold;
  never a headline from a single run.
- **Verdict rules.** Local rule with the same intent as S-1 (the shared
  `simulations/verdict.py` computes a Student-t interval of one mean against one
  threshold and has no Wilson or composite P1/P2 rule, so as in S-1 it is not the
  rule here): bootstrap 95% CI (10000 resamples across models, percentile) for
  means; Wilson 95% interval for proportions.
  - **P1 SUPPORTED** only if, for all six cells (3 patterns x 2 amplitudes), the
    proportion of models with |F_pattern - F_reg20| <= 10 points is ≥0.80 **and**
    the Wilson interval's lower bound is ≥0.80. **REFUTED** if any cell has
    population-mean difference ≥ +20 points and the bootstrap CI lower bound >
    +10 points. **INCONCLUSIVE** if, in any cell, the Wilson interval spans 0.80
    or a mean difference ≥ +20 has a CI lower bound ≤ +10 (and nothing is
    refuted). Otherwise **NOT SUPPORTED, NOT REFUTED**.
  - **P2 SUPPORTED** if the proportion of models with F80 ≥ F20 - 0.01 (a
    one-point tolerance for numerical noise; the literal count is also reported)
    is ≥0.90 with Wilson lower bound ≥0.90, at both amplitudes. **REFUTED** if,
    among models with F20 ≥ 0.05, more than half show (F20 - F80)/F20 ≥ 0.20 and
    the Wilson lower bound of that proportion exceeds 0.50. **INCONCLUSIVE** if
    either interval spans its threshold.
  - **Convergence:** on 10 models, regular 20 Hz, 80 Hz and burst at 1.5x, 10 s
    windows, dt = 0.025 against 0.0125 ms. Pass if the median |dF| ≤ 0.02 and no
    pattern-versus-rate classification flips; otherwise INCONCLUSIVE.
- **Gating of conditional readings (S-1's lesson).** When any gate below fails,
  the report prints no bold verdict word for P1 or P2: it prints "NO VERDICT" and
  a plainly labelled "conditional reading, not a verdict" sentence. Every F is
  accompanied by the scored / tested counts.
- **Declared limits (S-1):** says nothing about chronic decay of filtering over
  weeks, nor whether clinical amplitudes reach C-neurons at all (Graham/Lempka
  2019, 2022, PMIDs 30981900, 36387415 argue they do not); charge was not matched
  across frequencies in the human trials (n=19, n=17). The ensemble acceptance
  range for the unstimulated following frequency is 2 to 40 Hz (Gemes 2013's
  abstract, PMID 23148321, gives no numeric range; 2 to 40 Hz was chosen to
  bracket Sundt's 6 to 30 Hz, with a 2 to 15 Hz sensitivity cut; Gemes's table
  has the real numbers and was not consulted).

## Gates: what must hold before P1 and P2 may be read

All three gates are required. If any fails, P1 and P2 are **NO VERDICT**.

- **G1, calibration reproduced.** The selected set(s) reproduce all three Sundt
  bands at dt = 0.025 ms **and** at dt = 0.0125 ms, and the SK-removed mutation of
  each selected set leaves the 4.5 to 7.5 Hz band (the mutation check, as S-1).
- **G2, population progressive block** (S-1's Chao criteria, verbatim, with the
  onset floor added): at regular 20 Hz, 1.5x, on the accepted population, (a)
  time-to-block defined and ≤ 20 s in ≥50% of models, (a') median onset >= 2 s,
  (b) F in the last 10 s exceeding F in the first 5 s by ≥ 20 points in the
  population median, and (c) the Aβ population mean F < 5% with the bootstrap CI
  upper bound < 5%. **G2 is a calibration reproduction at population level, not
  independent evidence**, because T4 was tuned; it checks that the ensemble
  around the selected sets keeps the property. It is reported as such.
- **G3, held-out validation (V1 and V2), the independent test.** Measured on the
  accepted population at 1.5x:
  - **V1.** Per model, dF(f) = F(regular f Hz) - F(probe-only baseline), 2 Hz
    probe. **V1 passes** if (i) the proportion of models with dF(4 Hz) ≥ 0.20 is
    > 0.50 with Wilson lower bound > 0.50, and (ii) the proportion of models with
    dF(2 Hz) ≤ 0.10 is > 0.50 with Wilson lower bound > 0.50. **V1 fails** if
    either proportion's Wilson upper bound is < 0.50. Otherwise **V1 is
    inconclusive**.
  - **V2.** Per model, ff(2 pulses) ≥ 1.12 x ff(21 pulses) (one grid step). **V2
    passes** if the proportion is ≥ 0.90 with Wilson lower bound ≥ 0.85; fails if
    the Wilson upper bound is < 0.85; otherwise inconclusive.
  - **G3 passes** only if V1 and V2 both pass.

## Tuning search space (the search may use T1 to T4 only)

**Free parameters** (all others fixed as in `tjmodel.C_BASE`: stem length 75 um,
axon diameters, segmentation, Ra, ek = -90 mV, 35 C, Na shifts, M shift):

| Parameter | Range | Scale | Reference values |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nav` (axon, stem, junction Na density, S/cm2) | 0.034 to 0.043 | linear | S-1 0.0385; Sundt 0.04 |
| `nav_soma` (soma Na density) | 0.008 to 0.040 | linear | S-1 0.020 |
| `kv` (Kdr density) | 0.030 to 0.055 | linear | 0.04 |
| `kcnq` (M density, S/cm2) | 0.0003 to 0.004 | log | S-1 0.0015; Sundt Fig 5d 0.0004 |
| `gsk` (SK density) | 0.0005 to 0.060 | log | S-1 0.02; Sundt Fig 8c 0.001 |
| `gcal` (Ca influx, L-type density) | 0.00005 to 0.0020 | log | default 0.00025 |
| `sk_tau` (Ca decay, ms) | 300 to 20000 | log | S-1 1500; Sundt Fig 8c 500 |
| `diam_stem` (um) | 1.2 to 1.6 | linear | 1.4 |
| `sk_stem` (SK and Ca channel in the stem as well as the soma) | {soma only, soma plus stem} | categorical, equal | S-1 soma only |

The `sk_tau` range extends to 20 s so that a Ca decay on the time scale of the
Chao block is inside the space; `gcal` and `gsk` span 40x and 120x. The three
Sundt configurations share every parameter except those they remove: Na+Kdr sets
`kcnq` = 0 and `gsk` = 0; +KCNQ/M sets `gsk` = 0; +SK uses all.

**Search method.** Round 1: a Latin hypercube of N1 = 240 candidates over the
eight continuous parameters (numpy, seed 20261117, stratified permutation per
dimension), with `sk_stem` set by an independent fair coin per candidate (seed
20261118). Each candidate is evaluated in a funnel with early exit:

1. following frequency of the Na+Kdr configuration, in its band? else stop;
2. following frequency of the +KCNQ/M configuration, in its band? else stop;
3. following frequency of the +SK configuration (the "SK configuration" is the full
   candidate); recorded. If it lies in 2 to 40 Hz (the S-1 acceptance range), run
   the calibration run: from-rest and drive thresholds, then regular 20 Hz at
   1.5x of the drive threshold for 30 s with the 2 Hz probe (the S-1 protocol), and
   score B1, B2, B3. (The calibration run is thus done for every candidate that
   passes the first two bands and has a plausible SK-configuration frequency, a
   superset of those meeting T3, so that the report can say what trade-off the
   space contains.)

A candidate **meets the calibration targets** iff its three following
frequencies are in their bands and B1, B2 and B3 hold.

Round 2 runs only if fewer than 3 round-1 candidates meet the targets. It is a
local Latin hypercube of N2 = 96 (32 around each of up to 3 centres), centred on
the three round-1 candidates with the lowest score J (below) among those that
have a calibration run, in a box of +/-15% of each parameter's range (on its own
scale), clipped to the global range, with `sk_stem` fixed to the centre's value.
If fewer than 3 round-1 candidates have a calibration run, the centres are those
that have one (and round 2 is skipped if there are none).

**Selection criterion (calibration targets only).** J = sum of four terms.
Three Sundt terms d_i^2, with d_i = ln(ff_i / target_i) / ln(1.25) (1 at a band
edge). Block shortfall e1^2 + e2^2 + e3^2, with e1 = max(0, F_early - 0.50)/0.50,
e2 = max(0, 0.80 - F_late)/0.80, and e3 = 0 if B3 holds and 1 otherwise (missing
F_early or F_late: e1 or e2 = 1). J never uses any P1 or P2 quantity, F at 80 Hz,
or any pattern condition other than regular 20 Hz at 1.5x. Among candidates that
meet the targets, the **selected sets** are the (up to) three with the lowest J
that also pass G1's dt = 0.0125 ms check and mutation check, in that order. If
none meets the targets after round 2, the result is the sentence below.

**If no set meets the targets, the result is exactly this:** "No tuning of this
model class within the declared space produces progressive block with Sundt
following frequencies." In that case no ensemble, no pattern run and no P1 or P2
reading are made, and the report states, from data, how many candidates passed
each stage of the funnel and the best J with its components.

**Compute budget.** Search: at most 336 candidates (240 + 96), 12 workers or
fewer. Ensemble and all later stages as in S-1 (about 160 minutes of wall-clock
there, including a restart); expected total for S-1b is several hours. If the
budget cannot be met, any reduction is a disclosed amendment made without looking
at P1 or P2 quantities.

## Ensemble (only if a set is selected)

≥200 accepted C-neuron variants around the selected set(s): variant i uses
selected set (i mod K), with: `nav` x U(0.97, 1.03), `nav_soma` x U(0.90, 1.10),
`kv` x U(0.95, 1.05), `diam_stem` x U(0.93, 1.07), and `kcnq`, `gsk`, `gcal`,
`sk_tau` each x exp(U(ln 0.67, ln 1.5)); `sk_stem` as the set's (seed
30000 + i). Accepted iff the SK-configuration following frequency is in 2 to 40 Hz
(sensitivity cut 2 to 15 Hz) **and** the variant is drivable (junction-side stem
site). Candidates are considered in index order until 200 are accepted, up to a
maximum of 600 considered; if 200 cannot be accepted in 600 the run reports that
and the verdict is NO VERDICT. The subsample is the first 30 accepted models:
Poisson and jitter, 20 seeds, both amplitudes; the 10 Hz and 5 Hz probe trains;
the drive-matched 80 Hz diagnostic; convergence on 10 of them; 30 Aβ comparators;
a visual-traces file for the model closest to the median following frequency.
Stages, patterns and amplitudes are S-1's, plus regular 2 Hz at both amplitudes
(V1) and the two-pulse following frequency (V2) on every accepted model.

## Hand prediction, before running

**Do I expect a passing tuning to exist? No, probably not: I put it at about 25%.**
The arithmetic that makes me doubtful. Sundt's 6 Hz following frequency is for a
21-pulse train, 3.5 s long, and it fails because Ca (hence SK) has accumulated
within those 21 spikes. In a single-pool Ca model a 20 Hz train delivers the same
21 spikes in 1 s, with less time to decay, so SK reaches the same level at least
as fast; the block at 20 Hz should come at about 1 s or sooner for any Ca decay
slower than about 1 s, and for a fast decay (sk_tau of a few hundred ms) the
following frequency at 21 pulses is not about 6 Hz. S-1's result (onset 0.56 s,
all rates) matches this. The ways out are (i) stem stimulation that invades the
soma only partly, so that Ca entry per stimulus pulse is less than per orthodromic
spike (S-1's early pilots counted 1.5 to 3.7% of pulses producing a spike at the soma-side stem site; that is the site S-1's amendment H found to be on the wrong side of the stimulus, so I treat it as suggestive only),
(ii) SK in the stem as well as the soma, which changes where hyperpolarisation
builds, (iii) a very long Ca decay (10 s or more) with small per-spike influx, for
which the 21-pulse train would still have to fail at 6 Hz. These are real and not
excluded; the search will decide. A pass at the edge of the bands (6 Hz at 7.5 Hz,
onset just above 2 s) is the likeliest form of any pass.

Probabilities (my own, for calibration later):

- At least one candidate meets all targets after both rounds: **25%**.
- Given a selected set, G2 holds on the ensemble: 60% (variants are close, but
  the block depends on a sharp threshold in Ca).
- Given G1 and G2, V1 passes: **45%**. I expect stimulation at 4 Hz to amplify
  filtering of a 2 Hz probe in models with a 6 Hz following frequency, and stim
  at 2 Hz not to; the risk is that Ca decay of several seconds makes even 2 Hz
  stimulation accumulate.
- Given G1 and G2, V2 passes: 85%.
- So P(P1 and P2 are read as verdicts) is about 0.25 x 0.6 x 0.45 x 0.85 = **6%**;
  the likeliest single outcome (about 75%) is the "no tuning" result above.
- If P1 and P2 are read: P2 not refuted 85% (F should rise with rate once the
  block is slow, though 80 Hz may lose stimulus drive); P1 supported 45%, burst
  the likeliest violation (as in S-1).

## What would make me wrong in the interesting direction

If a tuning exists and passes V1 yet F falls from 20 to 80 Hz (a model that
blocks at 20 Hz and unblocks at 80 Hz through loss of drive or Na inactivation
upstream), then P2 reads as refuted for a reason that is not a T-junction effect;
the drive-matched 80 Hz diagnostic and the scored / tested counts are reported
for that reason. If the search finds block at 20 Hz only with a Ca decay of
several seconds and the 6 Hz following frequency is then reproduced only through
a different mechanism (the stem SK), that is a finding about which mechanism the
control needs, and is reported as such.

## Declared limits

As in S-1 and above: acute only; an intracellular stem current, which says
nothing about whether clinical extracellular amplitudes reach C-neuron somata;
charge was not matched across frequencies in the human trials; a
morphologically simplified neuron with one set of ion channels; no dorsal-horn
model and no ion-gradient run-down mechanism (Ladez et al., PMID 40039770, argue
the latter produces slow block; it is a rival mechanism and is out of scope).
Chao's block is measured by field stimulation in vivo; here it is an abstraction
(an intracellular stem current at multiples of a drive threshold). The held-out
features come from sources with the same mechanism class (Kent) or from
different preparations (Gemes, rat DRG recordings). Search-space limits are the
ones declared; "no tuning exists" means within that space and class only.
**These results are not stimulation settings.**

### Amendment 1 (2026-11-17, after commit #2 and one pilot, before the main run): pilot disclosure

Commit #2 (`47472f7`) holds the code. A pilot was then run through the full
pipeline, under the gitignored `results/pilot/` only, to find bugs and time the
stages: 16 round-1 candidates (T = 12 s for the calibration run), round 2 with 2
candidates, then a 12-variant ensemble around **S-1's SK configuration used as a
stand-in set** (because no pilot candidate met the targets; the stand-in is
pilot-only and is not a selected set), 8 s runs, 4 subsample models, 4 Aβ
comparators; 10 workers; 18 minutes in all. What the pilot showed, for the
record (none of it is a result, and none of it changes anything above):

- **Funnel in the pilot:** of 16 round-1 candidates, 8 passed the Na+Kdr band,
  1 of those passed the +KCNQ/M band, and that 1 reached the calibration run (SK
  configuration 19.3 Hz, outside the 4.5 to 7.5 Hz band; onset 1.4 s). Round 2
  (2 candidates around it) produced none. So 0 of 18 met the targets, and
  the funnel's yield past the second stage was low. A 240-candidate round 1
  could easily have only a handful of calibration runs; the "no tuning" sentence,
