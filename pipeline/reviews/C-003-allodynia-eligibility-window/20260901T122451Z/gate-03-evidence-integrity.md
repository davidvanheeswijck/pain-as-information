## Evidence Base Audit

### 1. WHAT IS MISSING
A hostile expert would immediately challenge the absence of three bodies of work:
1.  **C-fibre Selective Ablation/Blockade Studies**: The conjecture relies on the premise that ongoing, active C-fibre discharge is continuously required to maintain the transient "eligibility window" for allodynia. It fails to cite literature showing that once mechanical allodynia is established, selective acute blockade or ablation of TRPV1+ C-fibres (e.g., via QX-314 or capsaicin ablation) often *fails* to immediately reverse mechanical allodynia (e.g., *Shir et al., 1990, Pain*; *Campbell et al., 1988*).
2.  **$A\beta$ Nociceptor Literature**: The conjecture assumes $A\beta$ input is "individually normal" (innocuous touch). It omits the literature on specialized high-threshold $A\beta$ mechanoreceptors ($A\beta$ nociceptors) that undergo direct peripheral sensitization under pathological states, bypassing the need for spinal coincidence gating entirely (e.g., *Nagi et al., 2019, Science Advances*).
3.  **Chloride Clearance Kinetics**: To argue for a "few hundred milliseconds" window based on the Coull/De Koninck pathway, the conjecture must engage with the biophysics of chloride homeostasis. It omits papers showing that KCC2-mediated chloride extrusion recovery operates on timescales of minutes to hours, not milliseconds (e.g., *Rivera et al., 1999, Nature*).

### 2. NEGATIVE RESULTS
The conjecture is built entirely on positive reports of spinal microcircuits and peripheral imaging. It does not engage with negative results. Specifically, it ignores clinical trials of selective peripheral nerve blocks in neuropathic pain patients where local anesthetic block of unmyelinated fibres fails to abolish mechanical allodynia, which directly cuts against the requirement for ongoing peripheral C-fibre co-activity.

### 3. CHAIN LENGTH

### 4. QUANTITATIVE FIDELITY
The conjecture conflates the induction frequency of "wind-up" with the decay of "central sensitization." While the *inter-stimulus interval* required to trigger wind-up is indeed under 2 seconds (0.5–2 Hz), the *decay* of the resulting hyperexcitability and the downstream central sensitization lasts for minutes to hours. Stating that the facilitated state itself decays in "hundreds of milliseconds" is a quantitative mismatch with the cited literature on spinal facilitation.
