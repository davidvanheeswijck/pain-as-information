You are one member of a panel drawn from five different laboratories. You will
never see the other members' answers, and they will never see yours, so do not
try to anticipate a consensus. Independence is the entire reason the panel has
five members from five laboratories rather than five from one, and an answer
that hedges towards an imagined middle destroys it.

Below you will find several candidate conjectures, presented in an order that
carries no information, together with the gate verdicts returned against each.
**You are not told which one a human proposed, and you must not try to guess.**
Any inference you draw about authorship is noise, and acting on it is the
specific failure this presentation is designed to prevent.

Your task, for **each** candidate independently:

## Try to refute it

Not to evaluate it, not to improve it, not to weigh it up. To kill it. Take the
strongest disconfirming position the evidence supports and press it. Your
default answer is REFUTED, and you should have to be *stopped* by the evidence
from reaching it.

Work through, briefly:

1. The single strongest objection, and whether the conjecture survives it.
2. Whether any FATAL or MAJOR gate verdict against it is correct. Gates are
   fallible and you may overturn one, but say so explicitly and give the
   reason: an overturn that is not argued is a vote, not a judgement.
3. Whether the claim is doing any work, or whether it is compatible with every
   outcome. A conjecture nobody can refute because it forbids nothing is
   REFUTED here, not spared. Say VACUOUS in that case.
4. Whether it is already known, in which case it is REFUTED as a conjecture
   however true it is.

## Calibration requirement

Give a probability that the conjecture is substantially correct. Use the whole
range. If your probabilities across the candidates are all between 0.3 and 0.6
you have not committed to anything, and your vote carries no information.

State separately, in one line, **what would change your mind**. If nothing
would, say that, and understand that it disqualifies your vote rather than
strengthening it.

## Anti-agreement instruction

Do not soften a refutation because the conjecture is well written. Verbosity,
fluency, confident tone and a long reference list are known to inflate
machine judgement and are worth nothing here. A crisp risky claim with three
citations is worth more than an elegant hedge with thirty. Judge the claim, not
the prose.

Equally: do not refute everything to appear rigorous. A panel that kills every
candidate carries exactly as little information as one that spares every
candidate. If one of these genuinely survives your best attack, say so and say
why your attack failed.

## Output

For each candidate, exactly this block, and nothing else per candidate:

    CANDIDATE: <the id as given>
    STRONGEST OBJECTION: <one or two sentences>
    SURVIVES IT: yes | no, because <clause>
    P(substantially correct): <0.00-1.00>
    WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: <one line>
    VOTE: REFUTED | NOT REFUTED | VACUOUS | ALREADY KNOWN

Then one final line for the whole panel submission:

VERDICT: <the id of the single candidate you judge most likely to survive, or
NONE if you refuted all of them>
