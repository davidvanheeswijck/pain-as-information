## Problem
The evidence base (E-01 to E-08) covers how pain is encoded on nerves, how it
can be read and written, and what is known about CRPS and neuromodulation. It
does not cover the system that runs from the brainstem down to the dorsal horn
and turns pain transmission up or down. The independent review of 2026-11-16
recorded that the programme's work bears on none of the motivating phenotype's
questions, one of which is why the benefit of ganglion stimulation fades in
some people. Descending modulation is a candidate answer that the programme
has not examined.

## Proposed outcome
E-10: a literature brief, `evidence/10-descending-modulation.md`, at the level
of a published review, that answers:
1. What descending pain modulation is, in plain terms; what the conditioned
   pain modulation (CPM) test measures; and how it relates to quantitative
   sensory testing (QST).
2. How reliable CPM is: test-retest reliability, protocol dependence, and the
   consensus recommendations.
3. Whether CPM predicts chronic pain after surgery or response to treatment,
   with an evidence grade, sample size, independent replication and failed
   replications for each claim.
4. What is known in CRPS and in neuropathic pain, including sensory-profile
   clustering.
5. What is reported to change descending modulation: exercise, sleep,
   expectation and placebo, and drug classes.
6. Whether CPM or QST predicts response to spinal cord or dorsal root ganglion
   stimulation, or its loss of efficacy.
7. As a research question only, where common pain-associated variants act,
   and whether a sequence model could help prioritise them.
8. What this means for the programme: the hard core, the S-2 and S-2b
   simulations, a candidate conjecture checked but not filed, and questions a
   treating team might consider.

## Constraints
- EPISTEMICS.md rule 11: every query recorded verbatim with database, date and
  hit count; absence claims phrased as "not found by these queries" and
  cross-checked with a looser query.
- The abstract of every cited paper is read; quotes are verbatim; no number
  from recall; each number says what kind of quantity it is and in which
  population.
- ETHICS.md §1 and §3: population level only; nothing that reads as medical
  advice; implications for care are framed as questions.
- Anything not read is marked UNVERIFIED.

## Out of scope
- Congenital insensitivity to pain; ion-channel and growth-factor drug
  programmes; gene silencing or editing.
- Doses, stimulation settings, and any case-specific material.
- Filing the candidate conjecture, and any edit to other briefs, the ledgers,
  the README or the simulations.
