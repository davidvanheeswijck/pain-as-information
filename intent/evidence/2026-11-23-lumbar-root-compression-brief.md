## Problem
The evidence base covers CRPS, its diagnostic instability and the
re-adjudication studies in which a share of people labelled with CRPS turn out
to have another condition (E-05 §1), and it maps three questions about a
generator, about fading benefit of ganglion stimulation, and about allodynia
(E-11). In both briefs the alternative conditions considered are in the limb:
neuroma, entrapment and nerve injury. Neither brief asks whether a problem at
the lumbar spine (narrowing of the central canal, of the lateral recess or of
the foramen, or a disc herniation pressing on a nerve root) is found in people
labelled with CRPS of the foot or leg, as a mimic or as a contributor. The
foramen is also where the dorsal root ganglion sits and where a
ganglion-stimulation lead is placed, so the question touches the device as
well as the diagnosis.

## Proposed outcome
E-12: a literature brief, `evidence/12-lumbar-root-compression.md`, at the
level of a published review, at population level, that answers:
1. How often a lumbar or lumbosacral nerve-root problem is found in people
   labelled with CRPS, or with long-standing neuropathic pain, of the lower
   limb, as an alternative diagnosis or a coexisting contributor; and whether
   the spine was examined or imaged at all in the re-adjudication studies.
2. Whether CRPS is reported after lumbar spine surgery or with radiculopathy
   as the inciting event, with the evidence grade.
3. Which observations separate radicular pain or neurogenic claudication from
   CRPS-type pain, with the published diagnostic accuracy, the population it
   was measured in, and how common imaging stenosis is in people without
   symptoms.
4. What is published on compression of the dorsal root ganglion in the
   foramen as a pain generator, and whether foraminal stenosis is a stated
   contraindication, a risk factor for lead problems or a predictor of
   outcome in ganglion stimulation.
5. At class level only, what guidance says about MRI with an implanted
   ganglion or spinal cord stimulator, and which alternatives it names.
6. In one short section, what the major trials and guidelines say about
   surgery against non-operative care for lumbar stenosis, and whether the
   caution about operating on a limb with CRPS has been studied for spinal
   surgery.
7. What this means for the three questions of D-003 and E-11, with a section
   of questions a treating team might consider and a section on what the
   brief cannot say.

## Constraints
- D-004: the repository is an evidence resource only. This brief is an
  evidence addition. It registers no prediction, files no conjecture and runs
  no simulation.
- `evidence/README.md`, Maintenance, and the conventions of E-11: every
  load-bearing number carries exactly one source-status tag; full text is
  read where it can be; quotations are verbatim; no number from recall; the
  kind of number and its denominator are stated.
- EPISTEMICS.md rule 11: every query recorded verbatim with database, date
  and hit count; absence claims phrased as "not found by these queries" and
  cross-checked with a broader query.
- ETHICS.md §1 and §3: population level only; nothing that reads as medical
  advice; implications for care are framed as questions.
- Independence is checked per paper: author lineage and sponsor.

## Out of scope
- Doses, stimulation settings, advice, and any case-specific material.
- Congenital insensitivity to pain; ion-channel and growth-factor drug
  programmes; gene silencing or editing.
- Device-specific instructions of any kind.
- Any edit to other briefs, the ledgers, PROGRAMME.md, the README at the
  repository root, the simulations or the predictions. Corrections that E-05
  or E-11 would need are proposed in the pull request, not made.
